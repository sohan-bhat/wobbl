MARGINAL. Peak chamber pressure 4.14 MPa against a casing that yields at 16.8 MPa, a safety factor of 4.07 (target 2). J602 motor, 1238 N s over 2.06 s at 120 s Isp.

Confidence: medium. erosive burning: the narrowest port is 1.59 times the throat area at ignition and the peak mass flux down the cores is 2336 kg/(m2 s), against the usual limits of 2 and 1406. Fast core flow changes the burn rate, which this model leaves out, so the real pressure early in the burn can differ from this prediction

Assumptions:
  - KNSB (65/35 potassium nitrate and sorbitol), 1750 kg/m3, burning at r = a P^n by its published law (burn rate from Nakka, KN-Dextrose and KN-Sorbitol Propellants, Burn Rate Experimentation (nakka-rocketry.net/bntest.html), Table 2, with T, M and k from Nakka's KN-Sorbitol data as listed in openMotor's KNSB preset).
  - The burning itself comes from that burn rate law, not resolved flame chemistry. Erosive burning, ignition delay, throat erosion and slag are not modeled.
  - c* 908 m/s from T 1600 K, M 39.9 kg/kmol and k 1.14.
  - Thrust from the ideal thrust coefficient for k 1.14 and area ratio 6.35, times 98.9% for a 12 degree cone and a nozzle efficiency of 0.85.
  - Every surface lights at once at ambient pressure, and the gas in the chamber is combustion gas from the start.
  - The casing is checked as a long tube under internal pressure at room temperature strength. Closures, retaining rings, bolts and the tube's heating are not checked, so line the casing and size those parts on their own.
  - Against two MIT static fires in Wobbl's validation the predicted peak pressure was 7% low on one and 12% high on the other, so keep the casing's safety factor margin for what this model leaves out.

Suggested fixes:
