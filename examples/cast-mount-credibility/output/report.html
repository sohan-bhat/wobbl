How to fix it

Thicken the wall at the peak from 6 to 7.35 mm
The wall through the peak is 6 mm. To reach safety factor 2 it needs 7.35 mm (+22%), because bending stress falls with thickness squared.
Add a triangular gusset across the inside corner
The peak sits where two walls meet and bend. A gusset as thick as the wall, with legs about half as long as the shorter wall, ties them together and usually cuts the corner stress by half or more.
Switch to Aluminum 6061-T6
Stress stays about the same for the same shape and load, so Aluminum 6061-T6 (276 MPa) gives safety factor 2.23. It is 0.95x as stiff, so deflection scales by 1.05. Other options: Mild steel (ASTM A36) (2.02), Aluminum 7075-T6 (4.07).

Assumptions

  1. Linear elastic material and small deflections (no yielding redistribution, no buckling).
  2. Custom material (cast): E 72400 MPa, Poisson 0.33, von Mises vs yield 165 MPa (user supplied; the rest from Aluminum A356-T6 (sand cast)).
  3. Loads are static and applied all at once; no impact, vibration or fatigue.
  4. Cast part treated as sound and uniform; porosity or shrinkage in thick sections can lower the real strength, which is why castings carry larger safety factors.
  5. Fixed faces are perfectly rigid; real mounts flex a little, which lowers peak stress next to them.
  6. Remote loads and moments are spread over their faces without stiffening them.
  7. Contact between separate parts, friction and preload are not modeled.
