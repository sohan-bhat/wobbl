Assumptions

  1. A point mass in a vertical plane, so it does not spin, tumble or move sideways, and its drag acts against the air rushing past it.
  2. Drag coefficient 0.45 below Mach 0.6, raised through the transonic range to 0.765 near Mach 1.1 by a generic curve for a slender finned rocket, the same whether the motor burns or not.
  3. The 1976 US standard atmosphere from 0 m above sea level, a standard day.
  4. Gravity falls off with the square of the distance to the Earth's center, over a flat Earth that does not rotate.
  5. A steady wind of 4.47 m/s at every height, blowing back toward the start.
  6. Thrust follows the 75 mm L class curve in straight lines between its points, 3773 N s over 2.65 s, and propellant burns in proportion to thrust, so all 1.88 kg is gone at burnout.
  7. The rocket travels the full 2.44 m of rail before it leaves it, tilted 5 deg from vertical.
  8. Off the rail it turns into the wind it feels at its weathercock frequency, from Barrowman's normal force and margin and the pitch inertia of a uniform rod of its length and mass, with no lift at angle of attack and no overshoot.
  9. Each parachute opens over the time it takes to travel eight of its diameters, its drag area growing with the square of time, and the drogue stays on under the main.
  10. Under the parachutes only their drag counts, so the descent is slightly faster than with the airframe's drag added, which errs on the safe side for landing speed.
