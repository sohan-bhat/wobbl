Assumptions

  1. Laminate [0/±45/0]s, 8 plies of Carbon/epoxy T300/5208, 1 mm, plies listed from the inner face out, angles measured from the longest axis of the part.
  2. Carbon/epoxy T300/5208: E1 181000, E2 10300, G12 7170 MPa, nu12 0.28; Xt 1500, Xc 1500, Yt 40, Yc 246, S12 68 MPa; 0.125 mm plies (Tsai and Hahn (1980), Introduction to Composite Materials, as tabulated in Kaw, Mechanics of Composite Materials, 2nd ed.). nu23 0.49, the density and the 0.125 mm ply are typical carbon/epoxy values, not from the source.
  3. Plies are linear elastic up to first ply failure; Wobbl does not follow the damage past it, where the laminate often carries more load.
  4. Tsai-Wu uses the interaction term F12 = -sqrt(F11 F22)/2; Hashin's matrix compression takes the transverse shear strength equal to S12.
  5. Shell theory: in plane ply stresses only. Free edge, interlaminar and through thickness stresses, and delamination, are not modeled.
  6. The laminate sits on the midsurface of the CAD walls; the shell model holds 99% of the part's material in 1 wall faces.
  7. Points within 3.12 mm of supports and rigid attachments are left out of the answer.
  8. Loads are static. Fatigue, impact, moisture and temperature knockdowns are not applied.
