How to fix it

Drive the elbow through 2.5:1 and the shoulder through 5:1 with a belt or gear stage
Each servo then sees a fraction of the torque and turns that much faster, with 10% lost in the stage. Solving the move again, the busiest servo uses 44% of its torque at speed and the warmest 85% of its continuous rating.
Use a DSSERVO DS3218 at the elbow and a ROBOTIS Dynamixel XM430-W350 at the shoulder
Solving the move again with these, the busiest servo uses 39% of its torque at speed and the warmest 98% of its continuous rating.
Balance the shoulder with a 0.644 N m spring or 660 g on a 100 mm tail
Either carries about half the holding torque. Solving again with the counterweight, the shoulder uses 83% of its torque at speed and 233% of its continuous rating, and the base servo swings the extra mass, where a spring adds none.
Keep the payload under about 25.1 g
That is the heaviest load these servos carry through the same move over and over without running hot, found by solving the move at each weight. A single move without a stall works up to about 368 g.

Assumptions

  1. The arm has a TowerPro MG996R at 6 V at the base, shoulder and elbow and a TowerPro MG90S at 6 V at the wrist. Each can carry 33% of its stall torque continuously unless a rating is given.
  2. The move picks at 200, 0, 40 mm, lifts 80 mm, swings over to 0, 200, 40 mm and lowers, each step in 1.2 s on a minimum jerk profile, then holds 0.5 s. The heating estimate repeats that loaded cycle.
  3. The servos follow the move by computed torque, as a well tuned controller would, and a hobby servo's own loop lags a little more.
  4. Each servo's motor, felt through its gearbox, adds the inertia of a 20 ms mechanical time constant, typical of small brushed motors, since servo datasheets do not give it.
  5. The 500 g payload and the gripper are point masses at the tip, and the base is screwed to the table.
  6. Bodies are rigid, so links do not bend. A long or thin printed link flexes and rings more than this shows.
  7. Contacts are stiff springs that let parts sink into each other by the depth the contact check reports.
  8. Joints and gears have no backlash, and friction acts only where the inputs give it.
