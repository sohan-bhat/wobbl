Assumptions

  1. Wobbl guessed: friction coefficient between the clamped parts not given, so Wobbl assumed 0.15.
  2. Wobbl guessed: nut factor K in T = K F d not given, so Wobbl assumed 0.2.
  3. 20 n m of torque with a nut factor of 0.2 (t = k f d) gives 12.5 kn. Torque tightening scatters the preload by about 25% either way, so a torque wrench reading is not a measured preload.
  4. M8 8.8: stress area 36.6 mm2, proof stress 580 MPa, yield 640 MPa (ISO 898-1); head and nut bear on a 11.6 mm ring (ISO 4014 and ISO 4032).
  5. Each bolt is a spring with VDI 2230's stiffness of head, thread and nut, between rigid rings under its head and nut, with the bending and shear stiffness of its core. The shank does not touch the hole, so once the parts slide nothing but friction and the bolt's bending holds them.
  6. The clamped parts touch through penalty contact with friction 0.15 (VDI 2230 lists 0.1 to 0.23 for dry steel and 0.1 to 0.28 for aluminum). Embedding of the surfaces, which loses a few percent of preload, is not modeled.
  7. The hand calculation shares the load between bolts as a rigid flange would and takes it into the joint at the head and nut (load introduction factor 1), which overstates the bolt's extra load and understates the load that opens the joint.
  8. Materials are linear elastic and deflections small.
