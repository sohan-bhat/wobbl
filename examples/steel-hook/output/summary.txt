Assumptions:
  - Mild steel (ASTM A36): E 200000 MPa, yield 250 MPa, tensile strength 400 MPa (ASTM A36 minimum yield; MatWeb A36 typical properties). Past yield it follows a multilinear true stress strain curve: Yun and Gardner (2017), Stress-strain curves for hot-rolled steels, J. Constr. Steel Res. 133, quad-linear model through the yield and tensile strength.
  - The collapse load comes from an elastic perfectly plastic copy of the part at the yield stress with small deflection theory, the limit load of plasticity theory. Hardening usually carries a little more, while large deflections can carry more or less, and buckling or necking can carry less.
  - Hardening is isotropic, so reversed yielding as the load comes off (the Bauschinger effect) is not modeled. The load is applied once; repeated loads that ratchet or fatigue the part are not.
  - First yield is read away from sharp inside corners and supports, where linear stress is singular. A tiny region there yields first, which the plastic runs include.
  - Loads keep their direction as the part deforms, and supports are as rigid as in a strength study.
  - Wrought metal treated as isotropic; rolled, drawn or extruded stock is close to it at yield.
