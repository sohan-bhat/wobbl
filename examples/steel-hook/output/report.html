How to fix it

Deepen the section at the hinge by 1.1x
The hinge forms on face 6 at (6, -3.63, 20.2), where the part bends through a section about 12 mm deep. Its plastic moment grows with the depth squared, so about 12.7 mm deep (or 1.1x as wide) reaches the 1.5 target.
Use a metal that yields above 279 MPa
The collapse load scales with the yield stress, so 1.1x the 250 MPa of Mild steel (ASTM A36) reaches the target, such as Steel 4140 (quenched and tempered) at 655 MPa.
Keep the load under 0.896x this one
With Mild steel (ASTM A36) as drawn, the 1.5 margin on the 1.34x collapse load allows 0.896x the load in service.

Assumptions

  1. Mild steel (ASTM A36): E 200000 MPa, yield 250 MPa, tensile strength 400 MPa (ASTM A36 minimum yield; MatWeb A36 typical properties). Past yield it follows a multilinear true stress strain curve: Yun and Gardner (2017), Stress-strain curves for hot-rolled steels, J. Constr. Steel Res. 133, quad-linear model through the yield and tensile strength.
  2. The collapse load comes from an elastic perfectly plastic copy of the part at the yield stress with small deflection theory, the limit load of plasticity theory. Hardening usually carries a little more, while large deflections can carry more or less, and buckling or necking can carry less.
  3. Hardening is isotropic, so reversed yielding as the load comes off (the Bauschinger effect) is not modeled. The load is applied once; repeated loads that ratchet or fatigue the part are not.
  4. First yield is read away from sharp inside corners and supports, where linear stress is singular. A tiny region there yields first, which the plastic runs include.
  5. Loads keep their direction as the part deforms, and supports are as rigid as in a strength study.
  6. Wrought metal treated as isotropic; rolled, drawn or extruded stock is close to it at yield.
