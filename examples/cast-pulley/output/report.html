How to fix it

Make the riser about 1.1x wider
The risers freeze at 1.02x the casting's time and should outlast it by 1.2x. Freezing time grows with the square of the modulus, so a riser about 1.1x the diameter, or an insulating sleeve, gives that margin.
Steepen the freezing toward the riser
Porosity of 0.466% at (-4.48, 7.07, -6.66) mm comes from a shallow thermal gradient late in freezing. Taper the section so it thickens toward the riser, or chill the far end, so that region freezes in a clear order.

Sources: 0.5 W/(m K) and 1600 kg/m3 as in common foundry cost models (laser flash measurements give 0.34 to 0.4 W/(m K)); 1050 J/(kg K) is the 25 to 1300 C mean measured for silica sand in J.   5. The cavity fills at once with metal at 720 C, so the gating and the cooling while it fills are not modeled.
  6. Metal and mold touch perfectly throughout. The air gap that opens as the casting shrinks slows cooling late in freezing, so real freezing times run somewhat longer.
  7. Shrinkage is fed only along paths of metal that freezes later. Gas porosity from hydrogen is not modeled, which assumes well degassed metal.
  8. Risers are blind cylinders sitting on the casting and poured at the same temperature. The part is cast the way it is drawn, with +z up.
  9. Porosity comes from the dimensionless Niyama criterion of Carlson and Beckermann (2009), with the thermal gradient and cooling rate taken 10% of the freezing range above the solidus.
