  [pass] time step: half the time step moves the freezing time from 127 to 127 s (0.13%)

  [info] hand calculation: Chvorinov's rule with modulus V/A = 3.58 mm and mold constant 611 s/cm2 gives 1.31 min, and the casting freezes in 2.2 min here (1.68x), a spread that comes from a shape far from compact, which a single modulus cannot capture
  [pass] feeding: every part of the casting freezes before the metal on its path back to a riser does, so liquid can reach it as it shrinks

Sources: liquidus, freezing range, shrinkage and Niyama data from Carlson and Beckermann 2009; latent heat 403 kJ/kg as quoted for A356.0 by the ABM 2015 thermal analysis study (measured values run from 321 to 500 kJ/kg); density, conductivity and specific heat from MatWeb A356.0-T6; Scheil curve with k = 0.13 and the Al-Si eutectic at 577 C.
  - Silica sand, bonded: 0.5 W/(m K), 1600 kg/m3, 1050 J/(kg K), 26.5 mm thick around the part, its outside cooled at 10 W/(m2 K) to 25 C. Sources: 0.5 W/(m K) and 1600 kg/m3 as in common foundry cost models (laser flash measurements give 0.34 to 0.4 W/(m K)); 1050 J/(kg K) is the 25 to 1300 C mean measured for silica sand in J. Japan Foundrymen's Society 49 (1977) 747.
  - The cavity fills at once with metal at 720 C, so the gating and the cooling while it fills are not modeled.
  - Metal and mold touch perfectly throughout. The air gap that opens as the casting shrinks slows cooling late in freezing, so real freezing times run somewhat longer.
  - Shrinkage is fed only along paths of metal that freezes later. Gas porosity from hydrogen is not modeled, which assumes well degassed metal.
  - Risers are blind cylinders sitting on the casting and poured at the same temperature. The part is cast the way it is drawn, with +z up.
  - Porosity comes from the dimensionless Niyama criterion of Carlson and Beckermann (2009), with the thermal gradient and cooling rate taken 10% of the freezing range above the solidus.

Suggested fixes:
  - Make the riser about 1.1x wider: The risers freeze at 1.02x the casting's time and should outlast it by 1.2x. Freezing time grows with the square of the modulus, so a riser about 1.1x the diameter, or an insulating sleeve, gives that margin.
  - Steepen the freezing toward the riser: Porosity of 0.466% at (-4.48, 7.07, -6.66) mm comes from a shallow thermal gradient late in freezing. Taper the section so it thickens toward the riser, or chill the far end, so that region freezes in a clear order.
