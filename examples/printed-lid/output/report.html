How to fix it

Print in an enclosure near 45 C
The locked in shrinkage runs from the glass transition down to the air around the part, so air at 45 C instead of 25 C cuts the warp to about 2.86 mm.
Print it in PETG
PETG locks in 0.33% against 0.72% here, for about 1.75 mm of warp, if its strength and heat resistance suit the part.
Add a brim and keep the bed hot
While it prints a corner pulls up with about 339 N. A 5 to 8 mm brim gives that corner more bed to hold, and a bed near 100 C keeps the first layers soft and stuck, which this model assumes; it does not change the warp after release.

Assumptions

  1. Wobbl guessed: bed temperature not given, so Wobbl assumed 100 C, usual for ABS.
  2. Wobbl guessed: no enclosure mentioned, so the part cools in open air at 25 C.
  3. Wobbl guessed: print direction not given, so the part prints the way it is drawn, layers along z.
  4. Wobbl guessed: infill not given, so Wobbl treated the part as solid, as the calibration plates were.
  5. ABS: E 2200 MPa, Poisson's ratio 0.35, expansion 90 per million per C, glass transition 105 C. Tg typical of ABS (Stratasys ABSplus lists 108 C); warp ramp fitted to Armillotta et al. 2018.
  6. Printed with layers along z, into surroundings at 25 C (an open printer).
  7. Each layer locks in the shrinkage from the glass transition to the surroundings, up to 0.72%, rising from nothing at the bed to all of it 11.5 mm up, a profile fitted to the 48 ABS plates of Armillotta et al. 2018 (R2 0.94).
  8. The part is held flat on the bed until the print ends, then released; corners that peel during the print are not followed, so a part that lifts early warps more than this.
  9. The part is solid.
  10. Supports, the seam and the cooling fan are not modeled, and the layers are treated as one bonded solid.
