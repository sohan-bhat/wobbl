The agent knew the physics. Asked for hand calculations only, it landed within 1 to 5% on beam like parts. What it lacked was a fast, reliable way to turn that knowledge into a checked number on a real 3D part, and it kept hitting the same traps: meshers folding thin curved walls, singular peaks at bonded bolts, load idealizations that flip the verdict. Ask about a printed bracket. Get 52.8 MPa and a fix.

Through the MCP server, your agent inspects the part, picks supports that match the real build, solves, reads the checks before the number and asks for a fix. This is the shelf bracket example, driven through the tools.

Claude Code with Wobbl
  1. you

    Will this printed PLA shelf bracket hold its share of an 8 kg shelf?

  2. tool

    inspect_geometry("bracket.py")

  3. tool

    solve_structural(path="bracket.py", material="pla", fixtures=["bolt holes d=4.5", "wall x=min"], loads=["40 N down @ top and within 12 of 65,12.5,6"])

  4. verdict fails, 52.8 MPa on the 0.5 mm inside fillet, safety factor 0.95, confidence high

  5. tool

    suggest_fix(result_dir, apply=true)

  6. 3 mm fillet: 24.5 MPa (2.04); legs at 10 mm: 21.5 MPa (2.32); gusset: 10.3 MPa (4.84)

  7. agent

    It cracks at the inside corner: 52.8 MPa against PLA’s 50 MPa. A 3 mm fillet halves the stress; a gusset brings it to 10.3 MPa, safety factor 4.8. The gusset toe is a sharp corner Wobbl excluded as a singularity, so round it too.

The sharp corner cracks at 52.8 MPa. A gusset takes it to 10.3.

8 checks run on every strength answer.

They set the confidence, high, medium or low, and the reasons are always printed next to it. A result that failed a check is never reported as high confidence.

  • Mesh convergence

    At least three meshes, refined around the peaks that matter, until the stress moves less than 3%.

    44.2 -> 51.1 -> 53.4 -> 52.8 MPa over 4 meshes

  • Force and moment balance

    Reactions at the supports must cancel the applied loads to 0.5%.

    reactions balance the loads to 0.00011%

  • Hand calculation

    Beam theory through the critical section gives a nominal stress, and the ratio must be physical.

    15.2 MPa nominal, a stress concentration of about 3.5

  • Singularities

    Sharp inside corners come from exact CAD normals. Peaks there are excluded and named with their trend.

    14.8, 20.5, 23.1 MPa and still rising: excluded

  • Load introduction

    Off part loads are solved both rigid and distributed, each converged at its own peak, and the spread is reported.

    rigid servo: 55.5 MPa over 3 meshes, safety factor 2.97

  • Bolt bearing

    At bolted holes, reaction force over projected area replaces the idealized peak.

    13.1 N sideways, bearing stress 0.485 MPa

  • Units and sanity

    Part size, peak strain and deflection have to be plausible for the material.

    peak strain 0.015, deflection 2.8% of size

  • Large deflection

    When a part bends a good fraction of its wall, a nonlinear re-solve confirms the linear answer.

    runs only when the deflection calls for it

  • suggest_fixfixes with numbers, applied and re-run on request

  • solve_circuitLED, RC, buck, MOSFET or any netlist
  • list_materialsthe materials table

Where Wobbl stops today.

  • Linear elastic only

    It tells you whether yield is reached, not what happens after. Large deflection is checked with a nonlinear re-solve.

  • Static loads

    Fatigue, impact and vibration are not modeled yet.

  • Rigid wall contact only

    A part can push on a wall and lift off it. Bolted joints are clamped holes, without preload.

  • One material per study

    Assemblies of touching bodies are bonded together.

  • Thin walls are slow

    2 to 3 mm shells on 100 mm parts need large tetrahedral meshes and take several minutes. Shell elements are next.

  • Printed parts are idealized

    Isotropic at 100% infill, with a layer strength knockdown when you give the print direction.

Next up: shell elements, fatigue estimates and bolt preload in 0.2, CFD with OpenFOAM in 0.3, motion with MuJoCo in 0.4.

4 commands from nothing to a verdict.

  1. Install the CLI

    Python 3.10 or newer. It installs from GitHub until it is on PyPI.

    shell
    $ pipx install "git+https://github.com/sohan-bhat/wobbl"# or: uv tool install "git+https://github.com/sohan-bhat/wobbl"

  2. Install the solvers and connect your agents

    CalculiX and ngspice from conda-forge into ~/.wobbl, about 340 MB, no admin rights. Then the MCP server and skill for every agent it finds.

    shell
    $ wobbl setup

  3. Check every piece

    Solvers, rendering and each agent’s registration.

    shell
    $ wobbl doctor

  4. Ask about your part

    Inspect first to see the holes, fillets and face ids, then ask in plain words or with explicit loads and supports.

    shell
    $ wobbl inspect part.step$ wobbl part.step "will this 6061 bracket yield under 50 lbf at the tip?"
